AI Montage Guide for Reels & Shorts

Oct 19, 2026 · Youssef Mohamed Fathy

Let the AI do the captions and motion graphics, keep the quick cuts and B-roll for yourself in CapCut, and turn every good result into a reusable skill so the next video is faster.

MontageClaude CodeRemotionCapCut

1. Choose your style first

Decide how the video should look before any editing starts.

  • Browse Instagram Reels (or any source you like) and get inspired first.
  • Take screenshots and write down everything you want. Turn it into one document with ChatGPT, Claude, or any AI. Have your own style document before you start the montage.

2. Give the AI full context before it starts

The AI should know your direction before it touches the video. Share everything you have: style board, screenshots, examples, and notes on your audience and language.

  • Tell it what it must not change. For me: my footage stays exactly as filmed, no zooms or crops.
  • Ask it to send all its questions in one message before editing. Then agree on a plan for each moment of the video (a beat sheet).

3. Write your rules once in CLAUDE.md

CLAUDE.md is a file in your project folder that Claude Code reads at the start of every session. Put your permanent rules there so you never repeat them.

  • Real logos only. When a product is mentioned (Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor…), show its official logo, never a lookalike. Prefer SVG so every logo can be shown at the same size.
  • Save every logo in a folder. Download each logo once into a logos folder with a list of names and sources, so the next video reuses it instead of searching again.
  • Your non-negotiables. For example: footage untouched, no music (I use only light natural sound effects), caption language, and how every video ends.

4. Split the work: you in CapCut, the AI on graphics

The AI can do the whole edit, but a few jobs are faster by hand. Do those first in CapCut (or Edits, or Premiere), export, then hand the video to the AI.

TaskWhoWhy
Cut silences and repeated takesYou, in CapCutTakes a minute, and you know which take is best
Screen recordingsYou, in CapCutYou know exactly where they go; give the AI their timecodes
B-roll at specific momentsYou, in CapCutFaster to place by eye
Captions with key-word highlightsAITranscribes, times and styles every phrase
Motion graphics, logos, explainer pagesAIBuilds them from your style rules
Ending / follow call-to-actionAISame fixed ending on every video

5. Iterate, then turn the result into a skill

The first version is never the final one. Give specific feedback with screenshots ("this card repeats the caption", "this ends too fast") until it looks right.

  1. After every round, ask the AI to save the lesson as a rule, so the same mistake never comes back.
  2. Once a video looks good, ask it to turn the whole process into a skill: a saved set of instructions it follows next time.
  3. Ask it to add automatic checks for your rules where possible, so problems are caught before rendering.
  4. At the end of each video, ask what would make the next one faster.

6. Always improve

My montages were taking a very long time, so I asked the AI where the bottleneck was. It told me the slow part was transcription (turning my speech into timed captions) with a Whisper model running on my machine. I asked it to switch to ElevenLabs' online speech-to-text instead, and the montage became much faster.

The lesson: always ask the AI how the process can improve, and keep trying new tools. Something new comes out every month, so don't lock yourself into one setup.

7. My current setup (as of 18 Oct 2026)

PartWhat I use
AIClaude Code with Claude Opus 5.5, medium effort: works great for me
Manual editsCapCut (silence cuts, screen recordings, B-roll)
Graphics engineRemotion (animations written as code), set up by Claude
Final renderffmpeg lays the graphics over the original footage, untouched

Models change fast, so check which one works best for you when you start.
