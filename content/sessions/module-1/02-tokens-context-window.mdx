## Understand the raw material an LLM actually reads -- and its hard limits

Every LLM API call you make is secretly a tokenization problem. This session shows you exactly how your text gets split into tokens, why that changes how you write prompts, and what the context window limit means for your architecture.

<SessionSection id="big-picture" label="Big Picture">

## Tokens are the unit of everything -- cost, speed, and limits

Before the model runs attention or predicts anything, your text is converted to a sequence of token IDs. That sequence is all the model ever sees -- not words, not characters, not sentences. The context window is the maximum number of those tokens the model can hold at once.

## Tokenization is not splitting by spaces

Tokenizers use Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) -- a compression algorithm that learns common subword patterns from training data. Common words become single tokens; rare words get split into pieces. This means "unhappiness" might be 3 tokens while "the" is always 1.

### The context window is the model's working memory

The context window is the total number of tokens the model can see at once -- your system prompt, conversation history, retrieved documents, and the current message all count toward it. When you exceed the limit, either the API errors or old content gets truncated.

<SessionSection id="practical-example" label="Practical Example">

## Why a legal AI assistant keeps hitting context limits

A law firm builds a contract review tool. Lawyers paste full contracts -- often 15,000 words -- plus a detailed system prompt and conversation history. At roughly 1.3 tokens per word for technical text, a 15,000-word contract alone is around 20,000 tokens. Add a 1,000-token system prompt and five turns of history and the team regularly hits the limit on smaller models. Step through the simulation below to watch the token bar fill in real time -- and see exactly what the fix looks like.
