{/* SECTION 1 - GOAL */}

<SessionSection id="goal" label="Goal">

## Understand how the model turns tokens into numbers it can reason over

Tokens are just integers -- the model cannot compare or cluster them as-is. This session shows how embeddings solve that problem by mapping every token to a point in space where similar meanings end up close together.

</SessionSection>

## What we covered

<RecapTable rows={[
  { term: "Embedding", definition: "A list of numbers that encodes what a word means, not just which characters it contains. Words used in similar contexts get similar numbers — that similarity is learned automatically from billions of examples, not hand-crafted." },
  { term: "Vector", definition: "The concrete representation of an embedding: a fixed-length array of floats (e.g. 1536 values) where each number captures a learned dimension of meaning. Two vectors close in this space share meaning; two vectors far apart do not." },
  { term: "Cosine similarity", definition: "The standard way to compare two vectors. It measures the angle between them, not their raw distance — so a long vector and a short vector can still score 1.0 if they point in exactly the same direction. Scores run from 1.0 (identical meaning) through 0.0 (unrelated) to −1.0 (opposite meaning)." },
  { term: "Semantic search", definition: "Search that works on meaning instead of characters. Both the query and every document are converted to vectors, then ranked by cosine similarity. 'Car won't start' finds 'Vehicle fails to turn on' with a score of 0.91 — even though the two phrases share no words." },
]} />
